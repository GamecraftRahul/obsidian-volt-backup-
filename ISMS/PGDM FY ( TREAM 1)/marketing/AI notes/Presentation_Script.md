# Presentation Script — The Evolution of Marketing Thought
**By Rahul Kulkarni | 8 Slides | Target time: 7–9 minutes**

---

## Slide 1 — Title (30 sec)
"Good morning/afternoon everyone. Today I'm presenting on the Evolution of Marketing Thought — basically, marketing's entire midlife crisis, spread across a hundred years. We'll go from Henry Ford's factory floor, where he famously said you could have any color car 'as long as it's black,' all the way to 2026, where AI now knows what you want to buy before you do. Let's get into it."

## Slide 2 — Marketing Thought Is Not Fixed (60 sec)
"Before we go era by era, one big idea to hold onto. Marketing philosophy shifts because of three things."
- **Scarcity → Abundance:** "When supply is scarce, companies just focus on output. Once markets fill up with competitors, the focus shifts to persuasion, then to actually delivering real value."
- **Seller Power → Buyer Power:** "It used to be the seller knew more than the buyer. Now, before anyone buys anything, they Google it, check reviews, compare prices — the buyer has more information than the salesperson."
- **Intuition → Data → AI:** "Old-school marketing was gut feeling — a manager just guessing. Then came data. Now AI tools analyze that data and even generate the content itself. We've gone from 'I have a feeling this ad will work' to 'the algorithm calculated this ad will work,' which is a lot less romantic but a lot more accurate."

"Keep these three shifts in mind — you'll see them repeat across all eight eras."

## Slide 3 — Eras 1–4: From Factory Floor to Customer Focus (2 min)
*Walk through all four cards on this slide, one by one:*

- **Production Concept, 1850s–1920s:** "Make it cheap and efficient — demand will follow. Ford's Model T is the classic case — one model, one color, mass output. Ford literally said customers could have any color 'as long as it's black.' Imagine walking into a restaurant today and being told, 'You can order anything, as long as it's plain rice.' That was mainstream marketing back then."
- **Product Concept, 1920s–1930s:** "The belief that the best quality just sells itself. The risk here is what's called 'marketing myopia' — falling in love with your product instead of the customer's actual need. It's like a phone brand proudly announcing a 200-megapixel camera... on a phone where the battery dies by lunchtime. Nobody asked for that camera, we just want it to survive one full day."
- **Selling Concept, 1930s–1950s:** "After the Depression, factories had way more stock than people were buying, so the mindset became: push hard, sell harder. Heavy advertising, aggressive persuasion. You know that feeling when you walk into a mall just to buy milk, and somehow you walk out with a gym membership? That's the Selling Concept, alive and well."
- **Marketing Concept, 1950s–1970s:** "This is the real turning point. The idea flips completely — instead of 'sell what we make,' it becomes 'make what the market wants.' This is where the classic 4 Ps come from — Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Businesses basically had this shocking realization: 'maybe we should ask people what they want before we make it.' Revolutionary, I know."

## Slide 4 — Eras 5–8: From Social Responsibility to AI (2 min)
*Walk through all four cards on this slide, one by one:*

- **Societal Marketing, 1970s–1990s:** "A third factor gets added — society's wellbeing, not just profit and customer wants. This is the early root of CSR and sustainable marketing. Suddenly every shampoo bottle on the shelf wanted you to know it personally cares about turtles."
- **Relationship Marketing, 1990s–2000s:** "A loyal customer becomes worth more than any single sale. This is when CRM systems and loyalty programs took off. Your local coffee shop remembering 'the usual' the second you walk in? That barista has better customer retention skills than most of our group projects."
- **Digital & Social Marketing, 2000s–2020s:** "Search and social media hand real power to the buyer. Marketing becomes measurable in real time — clicks, views, conversions. And we've all had that moment — you say 'pizza' out loud near your phone, and suddenly every app is showing pizza ads. Is it listening? Marketing wants you to keep wondering."
- **AI-Driven Marketing, 2020s–Present:** "And this is where we are right now — predict intent, personalize at scale, let algorithms optimize the mix. AI can write ad captions now, which is a little concerning since half of us can't even write a good Instagram caption for our own vacation photos. I'll show you the actual numbers on this next."

## Slide 5 — Visual Timeline (30 sec)
"So visually, here are all eight eras laid out side by side. The pattern is consistent throughout — power keeps moving from the producer, to the seller, to the customer. And the tools keep evolving from pure gut instinct, to data, to full algorithms."

## Slide 6 — 2026 Market Snapshot (60 sec)
"Now, this isn't theory — this is happening right now, this year."
- "Global digital ad spend hit $835.82 billion in 2026 — 68.7% of all advertising worldwide. Source: GroupM's 2026 Global Ad Forecast."
- "87% of marketers are now using generative AI in at least one regular workflow — from Salesforce's State of Marketing report."
- "The AI-in-marketing tools market itself is worth close to $58 billion."
- "And AI-driven campaigns show about 22% higher ROI compared to traditional ones."
- "Search still leads ad spend at 40%, followed by Social at 32%, Display at 18%, and Video at 10%."

"And just to put $835 billion in perspective — that's roughly enough to buy every student in this class an unlimited lifetime supply of exam-week coffee. Several times over."

## Slide 7 — What's Next (35 sec)
"Looking ahead, four trends worth watching: agentic marketing, where AI plans and runs entire campaigns with light human oversight; retail media, the fastest-growing ad channel right now; privacy-first personalization as cookies disappear; and human-AI collaboration — the data actually shows hybrid teams outperform full automation. Judgment still matters."

## Slide 8 — Conclusion (35 sec)
"So to wrap up: every era we covered was the right answer for its time — none of them were 'wrong,' they just fit their moment. Even the guy in the 1930s who wouldn't stop calling you about life insurance — he wasn't crazy, he was just doing what the era demanded. The long-term direction has always been the same — more power to the customer, more reliance on data. And today, AI isn't the future of marketing — it's already the current chapter. The real advantage from here belongs to whoever pairs AI's scale with human judgment — because no algorithm can out-argue your uncle on why he doesn't need the extended warranty. Thank you."

### Timing Note
At a natural pace this runs about 7.5–9 minutes. Slides 3 and 4 (the era grids) are the longest — if you're tight on time, drop one joke line from each and you'll save close to a minute.

### Delivery Note
Let the funny lines land as a quick aside, then move straight into the next point — don't pause waiting for a laugh. On slides 3 and 4, physically point to each card as you talk about it so the class can follow along visually.
